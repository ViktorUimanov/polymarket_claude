## Rules (Apply Before Every Politics Bet)

### Rule 1 — Use Multiple Aggregators, Never Single Poll
**Rule**: Never base a political probability estimate on a single poll. Use aggregators:
- FiveThirtyEight / Nate Silver's Substack (US elections)
- Metaculus (geopolitical events)
- Manifold Markets (crowd wisdom)
- PredictIt / Kalshi (market-based)
**Apply when**: Any election or vote-based market.

### Rule 2 — Status Quo Has More Inertia Than Markets Price
**Rule**: Markets systematically overestimate the probability of dramatic political change in the short term (< 3 months):
- Ceasefires that "seem imminent" rarely materialize on schedule
- Regime collapses require sustained, organized opposition
- Policy reversals face significant institutional friction
**Implication**: On markets for rapid change ("ceasefire by month's end", "leader resigns by X"), lean toward NO unless change is already well underway.
**Calibration**: "Status quo" wins ~70% of the time vs market-implied ~40%.

### Rule 4 — Black Swan Budgets for Geopolitics
**Rule**: For any geopolitical binary (will X happen by Y), give a minimum 3% probability to unexpected reversals regardless of how certain the situation seems. Never go above 90% or below 5% on geopolitical markets.

### Rule 5 — Resolution Criteria Often Ambiguous for Politics
**Rule**: Before betting any political market, read the full resolution criteria. Many geopolitical markets resolve based on specific definition of events ("official ceasefire announcement" vs "cessation of hostilities") that may differ from commonsense interpretation.
**Apply when**: Every politics/geopolitics bet. Always check how Polymarket admin resolves this type.

### Rule 6 — Proportional Elections: Tightening Polls Signal Market Overconfidence
**Date learned**: 2026-03-16 (Slovenia SDS trade)
**Rule**: In proportional parliamentary elections, a market price of 80%+ for one party to win the plurality should be questioned when recent polls show the race tightening to within polling-error range. A statistical tie in polling does not support 80%+ market confidence.
**Why**: Slovenia — market at 81% YES for SDS despite one March poll showing GS briefly ahead (24.1% GS vs 23.2% SDS, a statistical tie within MOE). In a proportional system, 3rd-party performance and late swings can flip the plurality.
**Apply when**: Any proportional election market where the leader's margin in polls is less than 5pp and the market price is above 75%.

### Rule 7 — Election Markets: 30+ Day Horizon — Model Trump Endorsement as Binary Catalyst
**Date learned**: 2026-03-17 (Paxton Texas Senate trade)
**Rule**: For US Republican primary races with a pending Trump endorsement, model the endorsement as a binary scenario. Estimate: P(Trump endorses candidate A) × P(A wins | endorsement) + P(Trump endorses B) × P(A wins | B endorsed) + P(no endorsement) × P(A wins | no endorsement). Apply specific historical win-rate data for Trump-endorsed vs non-endorsed MAGA-aligned candidates in TX GOP primaries.
**Why**: Paxton market at 38.5% despite Paxton leading in post-primary polls (45-42%) and strong MAGA alignment. The market is pricing Trump endorsement risk heavily. The three-scenario model gives 52% fair value vs 38.5% market — a 13.5pp edge.
**Apply when**: Any primary race with a high-profile pending endorsement from a dominant party figure.

### Rule 8 — Principal Statements Override Secondary Source Denials
**Date learned**: 2026-03-17 (Trump China exit trade)
**Rule**: When a principal actor (President, head of state, CEO) makes a direct statement on record that changes the thesis, update fair value immediately, even if a secondary spokesperson contradicts them.
**Why**: Trump explicitly said he asked China to delay the visit by "a month or so." Bessent denied it the same day. We correctly weighted Trump's own statement over Bessent's denial and exited at +$70.40 profit. The market continued pricing 48.5% YES despite the president's own delay request.
**Apply when**: Any position where a principal actor has spoken directly on the key variable — weight their statement at Strong (±8–15pp) to Very Strong (±15–25pp) depending on specificity.

**Rule**: When holding multiple NO positions on Iran-related outcomes (ceasefire, regime fall, escalation), treat the entire Iran exposure as one correlated macro scenario. The combined loss scenario is "ceasefire occurs or regime collapses" — a single event that hits all positions simultaneously. Cap total Iran direct exposure at $600 (6% bankroll) regardless of individual edges.
**Why**: All three Iran NO positions (ceasefire Apr 30, regime June, regime 2027) share the same tail risk: a sudden diplomatic resolution or regime collapse would likely affect all three simultaneously. Individual edges of +17–29pp are real but the correlation means portfolio risk is higher than independent 2% bets.
**Apply when**: Any time adding a new Iran-related NO position while already holding 2+ Iran NOs.

### Rule 10 — Resolution Criteria Distinguish "Regime Fall" from "Leadership Change"
**Date learned**: 2026-03-17 (from Iran regime 2027 NO trade research)
**Rule**: For "regime fall" markets, read the exact resolution criteria. Most Polymarket regime-fall markets require dissolution of core state structures (Supreme Leader office + Guardian Council + IRGC clerical authority simultaneously). A new Supreme Leader, IRGC consolidation, or government reshuffling does NOT trigger YES.
**Why**: The March 9, 2026 installation of Mojtaba Khamenei as Supreme Leader was widely reported as "succession" and "instability" — yet it did not and would not trigger resolution on regime-fall markets. The market priced this as destabilizing (YES at 39.5%) when the institutional structures remained intact.
**Apply when**: Any "regime fall", "government collapse", or "leader ousted" binary market. Always read the full resolution criteria before estimating probability.

### Rule 11 — Proportional Elections: Require 3+ Independent Polls From Different Pollsters Before Entering Against the Frontrunner
**Date learned**: 2026-03-18 (from Slovenia SDS exit — trade file: `output/trades/2026-03-18_slovenia-sds-exit.md`)
**Rule**: Before entering a proportional election bet against the market frontrunner, require 3+ independent polls FROM DIFFERENT POLLING HOUSES all showing a statistical tie or tightening race. A single outlier poll showing the race as a tie is insufficient for entry — even from a reputable pollster.
**European election specific**: For European parliamentary and legislative elections (EU member states, non-US), polling ecosystems are smaller (often only 3-6 active pollsters total), individual polls have higher house effects, and poll aggregators are less mature than US equivalents. This makes single-poll signals especially unreliable. Always check: (a) how many different polling organizations have published recently, (b) whether the outlier uses a different methodology (online panel vs phone, weighted vs unweighted), (c) whether the outlier is from the same house as the prior outlier.
**Minimum evidence threshold for entry**: 3+ polls from 3+ different pollsters, all published within the last 14 days, all showing a lead of ≤ 5pp for the frontrunner.
**Exit sub-rule**: If subsequent polling reverts to the frontrunner showing a solid lead (5+ points) AFTER entry, exit at break-even rather than holding for the tail outcome. The thesis has been invalidated by the broader polling universe.
**What happened**: Entered Slovenia SDS NO at 19¢ based on one Ipsos poll (GS 24.1% vs SDS 23.2%, statistical tie). Exited at break-even two days later when 7 of 8 subsequent polls showed SDS with a 2.1–8.5pp lead (Mediana: SDS 30.9% vs Svoboda 22.4%). The single poll was an outlier, not a trend. Market at 81% YES was approximately correctly priced.
**Error classification**: information_error — insufficient polling universe at entry. The broader polling consensus was not consulted before bet.
**Apply when**: Any proportional parliamentary election where you are considering betting against the market favorite because recent polls look close. Extra caution for European elections where polling ecosystem is smaller.
**Rule 11 Addendum (Slovenia 2026 outcome, 2026-03-22)**: Even when Rule 11 triggers an exit, the polling consensus itself can be systematically biased. Slovenia final result: Svoboda 28.54% vs SDS 28.19% (0.35pp margin) — despite 7/8 polls showing SDS +5-8.6pp. This is a 5-9pp systematic polling overestimation across all houses. In small EU countries (≤6 active pollsters), all houses may share similar panels/methodology biases. A methodologically distinct outlier poll may have more signal than its face credibility suggests. **Updated partial-exit protocol**: In small EU country proportional elections, when polling consensus reverses after entry, consider 50% exit rather than full exit — preserving the original thesis while reducing risk. Full exit appropriate when consensus lead > 2× the structural mechanism buffer, or when 3+ polls use clearly distinct methodologies.

### Rule 12 — Sovereignty Acquisition Markets: Resolution Requires Actual Legal Transfer, Not Political Rhetoric
**Date learned**: 2026-03-18 (from Greenland NO trade — trade file: `output/trades/2026-03-18_greenland-no.md`)
**Rule**: For sovereignty/territorial acquisition markets, resolution criteria typically require official transfer of governmental authority — not military base agreements, framework deals, or diplomatic frameworks. Political rhetoric about "acquiring" territory is NOT the same as legal sovereignty transfer.
**Why**: Trump's Davos "framework" (January 2026) was widely reported as a sovereignty deal but was explicitly a military base expansion agreement. The market priced YES at 9.7% (10× the historical base rate for NATO sovereign annexation in 12 months). The legal path requires Greenlandic referendum + Greenlandic parliament approval + Danish parliament approval — three separate ratification steps, none initiated.
**Apply when**: Any market about territorial acquisition, annexation, or sovereignty transfer. Always distinguish: (a) political statements/rhetoric, (b) framework/base agreements, (c) actual sovereignty transfer with legal ratification. Only (c) resolves YES on sovereignty markets.
**Base rate**: Peaceful sovereign annexation of a NATO member within 12 months: <1%.

### Rule 14 — After Principal Statement Exit, Re-Evaluate Opposite Direction
**Date learned**: 2026-03-18 (from Trump China YES exit then NO entry — trade files: `output/trades/2026-03-17_trump-china-exit.md` + `output/trades/2026-03-18_trump-china-no.md`)
**Rule**: After exiting a position due to a principal actor statement (Rule 8), immediately run a fresh probability estimate using the new information. If the market, after partial repricing, is still mispriced vs revised fair value by >= minimum edge threshold, consider entering the opposite direction. The same principal statement that forced one exit can open a new entry in the opposite direction.
**Why**: When Trump said he asked China to delay the visit "a month or so," we exited YES at 48.5c for +$70.40. By March 18, the market had partially corrected to 39.5% YES — but our revised fair value was 25-32% YES. The market still overpriced the trip. We entered NO at 60.5c with +14.5pp edge. The principal statement was a two-sided signal.
**Conditions for re-entry**: (a) principal statement forces an exit, (b) market partially reprices but not fully, (c) revised fair value still differs from new market price by >= minimum edge threshold for the category, (d) no structural reason the opposite direction is already at capacity (correlation, cap limits).
**Apply when**: Any time a principal actor statement forces an exit. After locking the gain, immediately re-price the market fresh. Do not assume the market has correctly repriced — it may overshoot, undershoot, or stop partway.

### Rule 13 — French Municipal Runoff: First-Round Leader by 10pp+ Wins ~80-90% of Runoffs
**Date learned**: 2026-03-18 (from Paris Mayor Grégoire YES trade — trade file: `output/trades/2026-03-18_paris-mayor-gregoire-yes.md`)
**CONFIRMED**: 2026-03-22 — Grégoire won exactly as modeled. Final Elabe poll (45.5 vs 44.5, within MoE) was irrelevant noise. Structural factors dominated. Market eventually repriced to 83% before resolution, confirming the original 71.5% entry was genuinely mispriced.
**Rule**: In French two-round municipal elections, a candidate who leads Round 1 by 10+ percentage points wins the runoff approximately 80-90% of the time, especially when: (a) the structural electorate favors the leading bloc (Paris has voted left since 2001), (b) the third-party candidate in the triangular race draws from the same voter pool, and (c) no major scandal occurred between rounds.
**Why**: Paris mayor market priced Grégoire (37.98% R1) vs Dati (25.46%) at only 71.5% YES — 8.5pp below fair value of 80%. Market was incorrectly discounting the Bournazel-Dati merger and Knafo far-right withdrawal (which mostly reinforces Dati's votes but not beyond the structural left ceiling). The 12.5pp R1 lead + Paris structural left majority + LFI useful-vote dynamic (Chikirou voters go ~75% to Grégoire) produces an 80% fair value.
**Key sub-rule**: In a triangular runoff with a left-wing third-party staying in, the third-party draws disproportionately from the second-place right-wing candidate's potential coalition ceiling — NOT from the first-place left-wing leader. This strengthens the first-round leader's position vs naive two-way extrapolation.
**Historical precedent**: 2020 Paris runoff — Hidalgo led R1 by ~10pp, won runoff comfortably.
**Apply when**: Any French two-round election where the same triangular or binary structure carries over from Round 1. First-round data is actionable edge — markets are slow to price the structural implications.

### Rule 15 — Structural Electoral Mechanism: Require Polling Composite, Not Theoretical Tipping Point
**Date learned**: 2026-03-18 (from Hungary Fidesz YES exit — trade file: `output/trades/2026-03-18_hungary-fidesz-yes.md`)
**Rule**: Before entering a bet relying on a structural electoral mechanism (gerrymandering, winner compensation, single-member district amplification), require a POLLING COMPOSITE from 3+ independent pollsters — not just one aggregator — and only enter if the opposition lead is clearly within the mechanism's buffer, not "exactly at the threshold."
**Why**: Entered Hungary Fidesz YES at 33.5¢ when PolitPro aggregate showed Tisza +6.9pp. The winner compensation mechanism provides ~8-10pp seat cushion. The entry logic was: "Tisza at exactly the threshold." But deeper independent polling (Median: Tisza +20pp, 21 Research: Tisza +14pp) showed the lead was far larger than the single-aggregator number suggested. Exited at break-even when independent composite exceeded 10pp threshold.
**The flaw in "exactly at the threshold" reasoning**: Electoral structural advantages are size-dependent. A 3-4pp polling lead might be within the mechanism's buffer. A 6.9pp lead might be borderline. A 14-20pp lead definitely is not — and using a single aggregator that shows 6.9pp when independent pollsters show 14-20pp is an information error.
**Apply when**: Any election where structural advantage is the primary thesis (gerrymandering, winner compensation, incumbency bias). Require: (a) 3+ independent pollsters, not just one aggregator, (b) majority showing opposition lead < 0.5 × structural buffer, (c) no high-quality outlier from a respected house showing a lead twice the buffer.
**Buffer math**: Winner compensation mechanism ≈ +8-10pp seats above vote share for leading party. Enter only when independent polls show opposition lead ≤ 4pp (half the buffer). Do not enter at 6.9pp (the exact edge of the buffer).
