## Probability Estimate

### Base rate
Historical frequency of authoritarian regime collapse within 3–4 months of a major military strike: ~5–10%
- Examples: Iraq 2003 (yes, ~3 months), Libya 2011 (~8 months), Afghanistan 2021 (~4 months)
- Counterexamples: Iran 1980 (survived Iraq invasion), North Korea (multiple crises, never fell), Syria (survived 13 years of civil war)
- Iran-specific: survived the 1980–88 Iran-Iraq war with millions of casualties, survived 2009/2019/2022 protest waves, survived targeted assassinations of top generals

### Current evidence adjustments
- **IRGC intact** (strongest factor): The military structure is the regime. As long as IRGC cohesion holds, regime falls is extremely unlikely. → −15pp from base rate
- **Succession completed**: Mojtaba elevated. The regime resolved its leadership gap quickly without visible internal split. → −5pp
- **Protests present but fragmented**: No unified opposition leader, no defecting military units, no liberated territory. → −5pp
- **War consolidates authoritarian power**: Wartime "rally around the flag" effect within IRGC. Domestic dissent is easier to suppress when external threat is active. → −3pp
- **Economic collapse**: Could accelerate dissent but historically hasn't toppled the regime (2018, 2019 crises survived). → +2pp

### Final estimate
Base rate: 8% → Adjusted: **6–10% YES** (central estimate: 8%)

Market at 29.5% YES is pricing this at nearly 4× fair value.

### Why market is at 29.5%
The market is crediting:
- Unprecedented leadership succession (first ever, untested new Supreme Leader)
- Simultaneous: military losses + economic collapse + largest protests since 1979
- Tail risk: IRGC internal split if Mojtaba is seen as illegitimate

These are real factors. But for the regime to actually fall by June 30 (3.5 months), you'd need a rapid cascade: protests → military defections → IRGC split → regime collapse. History shows this takes months to years when it happens at all, and Iran's IRGC is far more institutionally loyal than, say, the Afghan National Army.
