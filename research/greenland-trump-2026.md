## Current Status as of 2026-03-16

### The January 2026 "Framework"

- Jan 21 2026: Trump announced a "framework of a future deal" with NATO Sec-Gen Mark Rutte.
- The framework does NOT involve sovereignty transfer. It covers:
  - Updating the 1951 Greenland Defense Agreement (US access to bases)
  - Expanded Arctic/NATO security cooperation
  - Raw materials access
  - "Golden Dome" missile defense deployment in Greenland
  - Countering Russian/Chinese influence in Arctic
- Trump withdrew tariff threats on 8 European nations in exchange.
- Multiple sources confirm: "no pen to paper, no deal on the table, nothing agreed to."

### Denmark and Greenland's Position

- Danish PM Frederiksen (Jan 22): "We can negotiate about everything politically — security,
  investments, the economy. But we cannot negotiate our sovereignty."
- Greenland PM: Sovereignty is a "red line." Full stop.
- Axios reported the framework "respects Denmark's sovereignty" — i.e., the US side moved
  toward Denmark's position, not toward acquisition.

### March 2026 Update

- No evidence of resumed sovereignty negotiations in March 2026.
- The Iran-US/Israel conflict (began Feb 28, 2026) has consumed US foreign policy bandwidth.
- Framework talks from January appear to have stalled or slowed per ABC/CBS reporting.
- Negotiations expected "in days and weeks" after January 21 — no announced resolution.

## Probability Analysis

### What would need to happen for YES by Dec 31 2026

1. Denmark/Greenland reverses hard "sovereignty is not negotiable" position
2. A formal treaty is negotiated (months of work minimum)
3. US Senate ratifies (requires 67 votes — extraordinarily unlikely in current Senate)
   OR Trump finds alternative legal mechanism (executive action — legally contested)
4. OFFICIAL ANNOUNCEMENT of sovereignty transfer made by Dec 31

Each step is individually low probability. Combined probability is extremely low.

### Base Rate Application

- Politics Rule 2 (status quo inertia): Status quo wins ~70% of "will X change?" markets.
  This is not a close call — both sides explicitly refuse sovereignty transfer.
- Historical precedent: The US has not acquired new sovereign territory since the Virgin Islands
  in 1917 (109 years ago). Each acquisition required years of negotiation.
- The January framework explicitly de-escalated toward a defense/access deal, NOT sovereignty.

### Other Prediction Markets

This market's strict "transfer of majority governing authority" makes 9.2% look HIGH, not low.

## Risks to NO

1. **Wild card executive action**: Trump attempts executive annexation or military action;
   even if legally contested, an "official announcement" might trigger YES resolution.
   Probability: ~2-3%
2. **Greenland independence vote + US deal**: If Greenland holds independence referendum,
   becomes independent of Denmark, then makes deal with US — possible path but requires multiple
   steps and Greenland has shown no interest in sovereignty-for-US-deal trade.
   Probability: ~1%
3. **Resolution criteria ambiguity**: Could a "declaration of intent" count? Resolution text
   says "officially announces that Greenland WILL COME UNDER US sovereignty" — could be
   interpreted as an announcement of future intent, not completion. Minor flag.
4. **Iran conflict spillover**: US geopolitical posture could shift dramatically; Greenland
   becomes less priority. Actually supports NO.
