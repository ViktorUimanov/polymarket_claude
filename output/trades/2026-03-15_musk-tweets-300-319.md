## Post-Mortem — LOSS (-$150.00)

**Error type**: model_error

**What I got wrong**:
My projection model extrapolated from weekday pace (45.4 posts/day over March 10-15) into the weekend and assumed linear continuation. The actual count stalled at ~247-248 with 18 hours remaining, implying Musk posted only ~20-25 more in the final day — well below the ~52 needed to reach 300. The weekend slowdown the market was pricing was real. My "some of Musk's most active posting is on weekends" counterargument was qualitative and not data-backed.

**Warning signs I missed**:
- By March 16 (late), the running count was ~247-248 with 18h remaining — the market had already moved the 300-319 bucket to 7-25% based on exactly this shortfall.
- The prior week resolved at 340-359, but that was a full 7-day window including two weekend days; this week's window started midweek and the first 5 days were predominantly weekdays.
- I noted the market may be right about weekend slowdown but discounted it as "uncertain" — this was the market correctly pricing known behavioral data I didn't have.

**What the market knew that I didn't**:
The xtracker.polymarket.com real-time count showed the tweet pace dropping materially over the weekend. Market participants following the live counter repriced from ~28.5% at entry to ~7% by March 16 because they had actual weekend pace data. I was trading on a 5-day extrapolation without updating when the live count diverged.

**Rule change**:
For multi-bucket tweet/count markets: do not enter without a live count data source. If a live count shows the running total falling below the midpoint of the target bucket with less than 24h remaining, exit or do not enter. Extrapolating weekday pace into weekends for social media activity counts is unreliable without historical weekend-vs-weekday breakdown data.

**Calibration impact**:
This is the first "other" category trade and a LOSS at 37.5% stated probability. The market ended up being better calibrated than me — they were pricing ~28.5% and the true probability was effectively near 0% by March 16 once the live count was visible. I was overconfident in my projection model for count-based markets. For future count markets: require live data source at time of entry; stated probability should be based on live count trajectory, not historical daily averages.
